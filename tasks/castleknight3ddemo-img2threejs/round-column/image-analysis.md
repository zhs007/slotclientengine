# Round castle column image analysis

## Identification

- Primary type: freestanding medieval architectural column; primary domain: object; confidence 0.99.
- Reference contains four consistent views of one radially symmetric stylized prop on a neutral background.
- Intended use: repeated real-time browser architecture prop, clickable/explodable by structural section.

## Overall form and silhouette

- Tall radial hard-surface structure with approximate height-to-shaft-diameter ratio of 6.5:1.
- Footprint transitions from a broad octagonal base to concentric circular rings and a tapered cylindrical shaft.
- Capital widens through rings, eight leaf-like brackets and a beveled octagonal top slab.

## Macro, meso and micro hierarchy

- Macro: stepped base; circular shaft; ornate capital; top bearing slab.
- Meso: two octagonal plinth courses; three torus-like base rings; five drum segments; neck rings; eight repeated capital brackets; two abacus courses.
- Micro: shallow vertical flutes; drum seams; beveled/chipped edges; sparse cracks; hand-painted stone mottling; cavity-darkened bracket gaps.

## Spatial relationships

- Octagonal base courses stack with butt contacts and decrease in radius upward.
- Circular rings overlap the base and socket the first shaft drum.
- Five drum segments butt vertically with narrow recessed seam bands.
- Capital brackets attach radially around the neck and support the top slab without floating gaps.

## Materials and finish

- Stone: opaque dielectric, mid-value muted purple-gray albedo, roughness about 0.88.
- Relief: shallow fluting, chipped bevels and sparse cracks; darker violet in seams and capital cavities.
- Highlights are warmer mauve on upward/beveled faces; no separate marble veining or gloss layer.

## Identity-defining features

- Clearly circular, lightly tapered five-drum shaft with shallow fluting.
- Wide two-course octagonal base and concentric circular transition rings.
- Eight repeated geometric leaf brackets around a broadened capital.
- Thick octagonal top slab with rounded beveled edge.
- Purple-gray hand-hewn stone with controlled chips and seam shadowing.

## Uncertainty

- Rear views are largely redundant because of radial symmetry; individual crack positions are not consistent and are treated procedurally.
- Exact capital leaf carving depth is inferred from three-quarter shading; it will be implemented as repeated extruded wedge brackets.
- The column will be scaled to the existing hall rather than treated as dimensionally exact architecture.

## Suitability verdict

Pass. The target is isolated, rotational structure is visible from multiple views, materials are readable and the component hierarchy maps cleanly to cylinders, torus rings, octagonal slabs and repeated bracket geometry.
